# E/S/G Pillar Check — Exploratory Analysis

**Exploratory.** This is a post-hoc breakdown of the paper's main ESG-disclosure measure into its three pillars, run once, not pre-registered, and not currently part of the paper's reported results. Modeled on the one-pillar-per-regression approach in Brasch & Eckert (2024, *Risk Management and Insurance Review* 27, 313-329) — with the pillar *definition* necessarily different, see the scope note below. Scripts and full numeric output live in `esg_pillars/`; this file is generated by `03_report.py` from the results CSVs, not hand-transcribed.

## Step 1 — Data availability (checked before building anything)

Sentence-level E/S/G labels **do exist** — `env_flag`, `soc_flag`, `gov_flag` are independent binary flags per sentence (ESG-BERT multilabel classification, CEO-letters notebook cell 40's `esg_multilabel()`), available for every sentence, not just ESG-flagged ones.

**But Table 5a's richer measures cannot be split by pillar without inventing a classification.** `Corporate_ESG_share`, `Verified_ESG_share`, and `Specificity_mean` all depend on a GPT specificity/corporate-relevance classification that was only ever run two ways: E alone (Section 6.11) and **S+G combined** (Section 6.12, explicitly filtered as `(soc_flag==1 OR gov_flag==1) AND env_flag==0`). Social and Governance were never classified separately at that level — the corpus was pooled by design. Building `Corporate_S_share` or `Verified_G_share` would require commissioning new GPT classification runs split by S and G, which was explicitly out of scope for this check.

**What this analysis actually uses:** the simpler `ESG_sentence_ratio` definition (Table 5a's Model 1 baseline: pillar-flagged sentences ÷ total sentences per firm-year), split by dimension. This is a real, unmodified measure already available from the raw ESG-BERT flags — not an invented one — but it is a **narrower** measure than the Corporate/Verified/Specificity-restricted pillar breakdown a reader might expect from a Brasch & Eckert-style table. If the richer version is wanted, the missing piece is new S-only and G-only GPT specificity classification (mirroring Sections 6.11/6.12's existing pipeline, split further), not anything this script can produce from existing data.

## Step 2 — Reproduction

`01_reproduce.py` independently rebuilt Table 5a (Q as DV) from the same raw pre-lag export used in `null_check/`, merged in the E/S/G pillar ratios, and confirmed **all six Table 5a coefficients reproduce within tolerance** before any pillar model was fit. Zero firm-years were lost to the pillar merge (0 of 209 missing an E/S/G ratio) — the pillar export and the main panel cover exactly the same firm-year set.

## Honest summary

**Five of six primary tests are informative nulls within ±0.10 SD; the sixth (`S alone` → `Q`) is an informative null only at the looser ±0.20 SD bound, not tighter.** Unlike the earlier `null_check/` result on the combined ESG measures — where the headline `Corporate_ESG_share` term (Model 3) failed to establish equivalence even at ±0.20 SD — every pillar here is at least loosely informative. That's a real difference worth noting: **this particular breakdown does not reproduce a hidden large effect** that the combined measure was somehow diluting. If anything, this adds modest weight to the combined-measure null being a real absence of a strong signal, not just underpowering — though "modest," not decisive, since these pillar measures are the narrower sentence-ratio definition (Step 1), not the same richer measure that showed the wider CI in `null_check/`.

**Do not read "5 of 6 informative nulls" as strong confirmation of no effect.** All MDEs here (0.08-0.16 SD at 80% power) are themselves not tiny — this design still cannot rule out small-to-moderate pillar-specific effects with high confidence, it can only rule out *larger* ones. And this whole analysis uses a genuinely different (narrower) pillar definition than the paper's headline `Corporate_ESG_share`/`Verified_ESG_share` measures, so it is evidence about *sentence-ratio* E/S/G disclosure, not a pillar decomposition of the paper's actual headline result.

## Assumptions and judgment calls (flagged per instruction)

1. **Pillar definition is narrower than Table 5a's combined measures** — sentence ratio (matches Model 1), not Corporate/Verified/Specificity-restricted. See Step 1. This is the single most important caveat on this whole analysis.
2. **Primary vs. secondary framing** — pillar-alone models treated as primary (per instruction, matching Brasch & Eckert's approach); joint three-pillar model treated as a secondary robustness check, not given equal billing.
3. **Standardization convention** — same partial-standardized-effect convention as `null_check/` (coef × SD(x)/SD(y) on each model's own complete-case sample).
4. **z, not t, critical values** — matches this codebase's HC3 output convention (`P>|z|`), negligible difference at this sample size (df≈147-148).
5. **TOST bounds (±0.10/±0.20 SD)** were specified directly in the request, not chosen by me.
6. **VIF/collinearity check scope** — computed on the joint model's own design matrix (pillars + controls), FE dummies excluded (same convention as `null_check/`, since categorical FE mechanically inflates VIF regardless of real collinearity).
7. **Multiple-testing correction applied across exactly the six primary tests** (not including the two secondary joint-model coefficients per pillar, which would double the family and change the corrected thresholds) — a defensible but not the only reasonable choice of test family.
